I build open data infrastructure that helps governments understand where things are and how they connect.

At TerraFrame I work across product, engineering, and operations, and directly with the customers who rely on what we build. In Marble, Colorado, I founded West Slope Technologists, a rural tech community that grew to nearly 300 people.

What I do at TerraFrame

TerraFrame is a small team, so titles only tell part of the story. I've worn most of the hats a small company has, from writing code to selling it. These are the ones I wear most.

Product lead. I define what TerraFrame builds and how we get there: product strategy, the roadmap, and how our platforms fit the people who use them.

Customer partner. I work alongside our CEO with customers, from first conversations to long-term partnerships with federal clients like the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers.

Operator. I run much of how the team works day to day: project management, sprint planning, customer and user support, and the operational tools we rely on.

Developer. I came up as a full-stack and GIS developer, and I still work in the architecture and the code.

The thread through all of it is translation. I sit with customers to understand what they need, often before they've fully put it into words, then turn that into work an engineering team can build. Much of my job is being the person a customer can call who understands both their mission and our technology, and who follows through.

What we're building

Geoprism Registry

Authoritative geographic data, tracked over time.

An open-source platform for managing what exists, where it is, how it's organized, and how that has changed. It maintains the shared location data that other systems depend on.

Geoprism Registry on GitHub

Kaleidoscope

GeoAI grounded in spatial knowledge graphs.

Instead of retrieving loose text chunks, Kaleidoscope uses a knowledge graph's structure and geography to ground AI answers in connected, traceable data. We call this approach Geo-Graph RAG.

Kaleidoscope on GitHub

Imagery Data Manager (IDM)

One place to store, process, and share drone imagery.

An open-source, cloud-based application built for the U.S. Department of the Interior. It processes drone imagery and lidar into orthomosaics, terrain models, and point clouds, connects everything by location and time, and publishes products through open standards like STAC.

IDM on GitHub

Geoprism

Data management and GIS on one platform.

An integrated platform for data management and visualization that runs on TerraFrame's RunwaySDK. It keeps data consistent while letting people build interactive maps and dashboards on the fly, and it's the foundation IDM is built on.

Geoprism on GitHub

Much of this work is part of the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers' Geospatial Knowledge Infrastructure effort, which USACE funds and TerraFrame leads as developer. Earlier, I helped build TerraFrame's RunwaySDK framework, and the Common Geo-Registry work for the Digital Solutions for Malaria Elimination partnership.

Community

When I moved to Marble in 2017, I started a small group to find the other technologists in the mountains. It grew into West Slope Technologists, a community of nearly 300 people across rural western and southern Colorado, and into a broader effort to build a tech ecosystem outside the Front Range alongside the region's startup organizers, rural-focused investors, and coworking spaces.

Along the way I organized the Rural Tech Retreat, a semi-off-grid conference in Marble. Starting in 2021, I captained the Technology Track at West Slope Startup Week, and I serve as a strategic advisor to CoVenture in Carbondale.
